GPU financing already has a market of its own: roughly fifteen GPU-backed facilities since 2023, rated by two agencies, with CoreWeave about half. It is an established product, not yet an industry default, and this call depends on it continuing. What is still undecided is the layer between the GPU and the switchgear that expires with each chip platform. Today it floats between the landlord's turnkey build, customer-funded rent-credit deals, and leasehold improvements. My call is that it lands in the equipment claim, because reserves can't price a system change, guarantees only exist where a hyperscaler stands behind the tenant, and faster amortization taxes the long-lived asset to protect the short-lived one. Renewals and refinancings in 2027–28 are where the documents get written. Sixty is a bet that those documents become visible, not only that they get written. If 2028 is mostly hyperscaler-backed, this loses, and it should.
