Demand for AI compute will keep growing through the window. That is not
the call. The call is that growth will not be enough to earn the returns
most projects now under construction were underwritten to. Three things
sit between demand and returns: GPU-hour price, which falls with every
chip generation and every lump of new capacity; depreciation, which runs
shorter than the models assume; and the small number of tenants who can
sign a bankable contract. You can have record demand and a bust at once:
fiber in 2000 is the example. So this is a returns bust, not a demand
bust. Chips won't sit idle; they'll rent for less than the pro forma
assumed, which is enough to break anything built on the pro forma.
